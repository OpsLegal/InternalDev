My Things: visual options

Five ways to show what you own, so finding a category, filling it in and seeing what needs care is quick. Each sketch is phone-sized and uses example things. Under them: the category set with the data each kind keeps, and how this feeds the long-term question "is this thing worth what it asks of me?".

The category set

Eight categories, each with sub-categories. Picking a sub-category pre-fills the right fields, tags, documents to expect and upkeep to suggest, so you type little. Everything links to its documents, expenses and tasks.

E · Worth it?
9:41Balance
What each thing asks vs gives
Best for: the long term.Each thing is a bubble: across = what it asks of you per year (tasks and money), up = what it gives back (the values it serves), size = what it's worth. The four corners suggest keep, worth the effort, review, or let go. It needs a few months of data to mean something.

My suggestion

  1. A · the wheel as the entry to My things: to add a thing and to find a category quickly. Sub-category chips pre-fill the form.
  2. D · the passport as each thing's own page: the right fields for its kind, documents as thumbnails, next care.
  3. E · worth it? later, once a few months of tasks and expenses exist, for the alignment question. It becomes part of the weekly review: one thing a month to look at.
  4. B and C stay options: B if the wheel feels too playful day to day, C as a view inside the Overview.

How "worth it" would be measured, by rules on the phone:

asks per year = cells × 1.5 h + quick things × 0.25 h · plus money spent
gives back = the values it serves (0–3 each, set once, like projects)
corner = high gives + low asks → keep · high + high → worth the effort · low + low → fine · low gives + high asks → review
